5 Email Flows Every D2C Brand Should Set Up First

By Digital Dobby · 17 November 2026 · 6 min read

Most D2C brands spend almost all their marketing budget finding new customers, then do very little to bring them back. Automated email flows fix that. You set them up once, and they keep earning while you focus on everything else.

Start with these five, in this order. Each one works on Shopify Email, Klaviyo or most other email tools.

1. Welcome flow

When it starts: someone joins your list, usually through a pop-up or at checkout.

What to send (3 emails over about a week):

  1. Thank them, deliver any promised discount, and say what makes your brand different.
  2. Tell your story: why you started, how products are made, what’s in them.
  3. Show your best sellers with real reviews, and remind them the discount is waiting.

Welcome emails are usually the most-read emails a brand sends, because people have just asked to hear from you.

2. Abandoned cart flow

When it starts: someone adds to cart or starts checkout but doesn’t finish.

What to send (2–3 emails):

  1. A friendly reminder within a few hours, with the product image and a button back to the cart.
  2. Next day: answer the likely objection (delivery time, returns, cash on delivery, how to use the product).
  3. Optional, two days later: a small incentive, such as free shipping. Don’t make discounts automatic, or customers learn to wait for them.

3. Post-purchase flow

When it starts: the first order.

What to send:

  • A thank-you with what happens next and delivery tracking.
  • When it arrives: how to use the product to get the most from it. This cuts returns and complaints, especially for supplements and skincare.
  • A week or two later: ask for a review. Reviews feed your website, ads and SEO.

4. Replenishment flow

When it starts: a set time after purchase, based on how long the product lasts.

If a tub of protein lasts 30 days, remind the customer around day 25: “Running low? Reorder in one click.” Add a subscribe-and-save option if you offer one. For consumables like supplements, food and personal care, this is often the flow that earns the most repeat revenue.

5. Win-back flow

When it starts: a customer hasn’t ordered for a while (for example, 60–90 days for consumables).

What to send (2–3 emails): what’s new since they last ordered, a reason to come back (a new flavour, a bundle, an offer), and finally a polite “should we stop emailing?” Removing people who don’t open keeps your emails landing in inboxes, not spam.

Getting the basics right

  • Consent: only email people who signed up or bought from you, and include a clear unsubscribe link in every email.
  • Send from your own domain (for example hello@yourbrand.com), with SPF and DKIM set up, so your emails don’t land in spam.
  • Keep it mobile-first: most people read on their phones, so use short emails, one main button and big images.
  • Add WhatsApp carefully: order updates and replies work well there. Promotional messages need opt-in.

What this looks like in practice

For Jollywell, a plant-based supplement brand, automated re-engagement emails were part of a wider plan with SEO and A/B-tested ads. Engagement from email rose 20%, alongside 45% more organic traffic. For InterFace, targeted email workflows lifted open rates by 25%.
