How Much Does Digital Marketing Cost for a Real Estate Project in India?

By Digital Dobby · 17 November 2026 · 6 min read

“How much should we spend on digital marketing for this launch?” is usually the first question developers and brokers ask us. The honest answer is that it depends, but it depends on a small number of things you can estimate before you spend a rupee.

This guide breaks the cost into its two parts, shows how to work backwards from your sales target, and lists the mistakes that waste budget.

The two parts of the cost

Every digital campaign for a project has two separate costs:

  1. Ad spend: what you pay Google, Meta (Facebook and Instagram) and other platforms for your ads to be shown. You should pay this directly to the platforms from accounts you own.
  2. Management fee: what you pay an agency or freelancer to plan, build and run the campaigns, landing pages, creatives and reporting.

Keep the two apart in every proposal you compare. A low fee with no ad budget won’t generate leads, and a large ad budget run badly wastes money quickly.

What drives the ad budget

Your ad budget depends mainly on how many leads you need and what each lead costs. Cost per lead varies widely with:

  • City and micro-market: competitive areas in Mumbai, Thane or Pune cost more per click than smaller cities.
  • Price segment: luxury buyers are fewer and more expensive to reach than buyers of affordable homes.
  • Channel: Google Search reaches people actively looking (“2 BHK in Thane West”), usually at a higher cost per lead but with stronger intent. Meta reaches people earlier and often costs less per lead, but needs better qualification.
  • Landing page and follow-up: a fast page and a reply within minutes can halve your effective cost per lead.

Work backwards from your sales target

The most reliable way to set a budget is to start from units sold and work back. Here’s an example with illustrative numbers. Replace them with your own.

Example: you want to sell 20 units in the launch phase.

  • If about 1 in 10 site visits becomes a booking, you need about 200 site visits.
  • If about 1 in 8 qualified leads visits the site, you need about 1,600 leads.
  • At an assumed ₹600 per lead, that’s an ad budget of about ₹9.6 lakh over the launch period.

These ratios and the cost per lead are examples only. Your own past launches, sales team and market will give you better numbers.

Run the same sum with a lower and a higher cost per lead to get a realistic range, then decide which number you can live with.

What the management fee covers

A good agency fee should cover more than “running ads”. For a launch, expect:

  • Market and keyword research for the micro-market
  • Campaign set-up on Google and Meta, with tracking that counts leads and site visits, not just clicks
  • A fast, mobile-first landing page with RERA details and one clear enquiry action
  • Creatives and ad copy, refreshed during the campaign
  • Lead qualification and an instant WhatsApp or email reply
  • Weekly optimisation and a clear monthly report

Our own plans start from ₹30,000 a month for management, with ad spend paid directly by you to the platforms.

Five mistakes that waste budget

  1. Sending ads to the homepage instead of a project landing page.
  2. A one-field form that anyone fills in. Add one qualifying question, such as budget or timeline.
  3. Slow follow-up. A lead that waits hours is often lost.
  4. Targeting too widely (“Mumbai, 25–55”) instead of the micro-market and buyer intent.
  5. Judging by cost per lead alone. Cheap leads that never visit cost more in the end. Track site visits and bookings.

What results can you expect?

It depends on the project, price and market, so be wary of anyone who guarantees a number.
